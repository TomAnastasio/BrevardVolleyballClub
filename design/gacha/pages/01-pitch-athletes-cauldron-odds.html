Part 1

Five Retro Athletes

Each body is drawn as zones: hair, skin, shirt, sleeves, shorts, shins, socks and shoes, plus a spot on top of the head and a spot in the hand. A shirt is just colors and a pattern for the shirt zone, so I draw it once and it fits all five. Hats are drawn to fit the head width, and held items snap to the hand. Long-sleeve shirts and long pants simply fill the arm and shin zones too. Tap "Show gear zones" to see the map.

Gear colors match their tier: grey is the free default, then Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary. The rival in the small rally wears default gear in a different color here; in the app they'd wear their own gear.

Part 3 · needs a decision

1 token = 1 pull changes the math

The odds you liked assumed 5 tokens per pull. At 1 token per pull, everyone gets about five times as many pulls. With the 58-item set, a weekly regular would own three quarters of it by the end of November with a 41% chance of a Legendary, and anyone playing twice a week would own everything. To keep the same feel, the set needs to be about three times bigger and Legendaries rarer. Commons can be recolors, so most of the extra items are cheap to make. Try both with the buttons.

Already decided

  • The Cauldron opens every pull. 1 token per pull.
  • 1 token per saved game, 2 for a win, at most 10 a day. Only Google-signed-in players earn tokens. No back pay.
  • Deleting a game never takes tokens back. Merging a placeholder profile into a Google account changes nothing.
  • No duplicates. Someone who owns everything can't pull, and their tokens wait for the next set.
  • Cosmetic only. Nothing touches Elo. No real money, ever. No trading. Pulls are rolled by the database.
  • Four tiers. Until the end of November every item is Halloween; after that the Halloween items are shelved for good and a non-Halloween set we make together takes over.
  • Your home-screen rally plays your rival (the app's existing "toughest opponent"). With no rival yet, you play the #1 ranked player.
  • Victory and defeat poses show on past games: winners celebrate on one side, losers slump on the other.
